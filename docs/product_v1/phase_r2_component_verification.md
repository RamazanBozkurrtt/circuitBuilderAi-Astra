# Product V1 Phase R2 component and interface verification

**Scope and result (2026-09-28).** The frozen product boundary is Board A (Controller + Audio AFE), Board B (Power + Stereo Class-D), four analog microphone inputs and two BTL speaker outputs. The four target ICs are **PROVISIONALLY APPROVED** for architecture. Manufacturer evidence supports a four-channel 96-kHz-class ADC path, a second stereo output path, and native RT1062 external-NOR boot. It does **not** approve the microphone analog circuit, pin map, boot policy, 12-V speaker power, safety timing, acoustic latency or schematic entry. R3 may decide the architecture; R4 must close circuit and safety contracts. Product authority is the [two-board product document](../product/ANC_B2B_Custom_PCB_2_Kart_Mimari_Tasarim_Dokumani_v1.0.docx) §§1–7, 12. The [R1 requirements](phase_r1_product_requirements.md) and [evidence index](component_evidence_index.md) control terminology. Legacy IC work supplies no electrical evidence here.

**Evidence convention.** `CONFIRMED` describes the cited manufacturer's stated condition, `PROVISIONAL` an engineering candidate or inference, `UNKNOWN` a missing value/test, and `CONFLICTING` a source-to-source or source-to-requirement discrepancy. Datasheet typical figures are not worst-case limits. PDF pages and tables below are exact evidence locations; E-IDs resolve in the index. R2 did not select a schematic, BOM, connector, or pin assignment.

## 2. Board A microphone and ADC

### IM73A135V01 electrical contract

`CONFIRMED` E-MIC identifies a bottom-port analog differential MEMS microphone, ordering code SP003803274, PG-LLGA-5-2, 4 × 3 × 1.2 mm. Pins are 1 OUT+, 2 VDD, 3 OUT−, 4/5 GND (p.11 Table 8). Its supply selects normal mode at 2.3–3.0 V (2.75 V typical), low-power mode at 1.52–1.8 V (1.6 V typical); 3.0 V is also the absolute maximum, so a nominal 3.3-V rail is **incompatible** (p.8 Tables 5–6). Normal-mode current is 170 µA typical, 230 µA maximum at ≤94 dB SPL; low-power is 70/80 µA (p.10 Table 7). VDD reaches its final ±10% within 5 ms; startup is 10 ms typical/30 ms maximum after VDDmin and output is undefined for 10 ms after mode change (pp.8, 10). Design for muted boot until all channels settle.

At VDD 2.75 V, 25 °C and unloaded output, sensitivity is −38 dBV typical ±1 dB at 1 kHz/94 dB SPL, normal-mode AOP 135 dB SPL at 10% THD, SNR 73 dB(A), 20-Hz −3-dB low-frequency cutoff (p.6 Table 3). At 20 Hz response is −1.5 to −4.5 dB relative to 1 kHz; at 50 Hz it is +0.5 to −1.5 dB (p.7 Table 4). Typical group delay is 52 µs at 250 Hz and 7 µs at 600 Hz (p.6); these are not a complete installed ANC phase/latency guarantee. The product's preliminary 80–800 Hz sweep is inside the published response band, so the microphone is defensible for a prototype. Actual location, cavity, port and harness response remain `UNKNOWN`.

Each differential output has 1.35-V nominal DC level in normal mode (0.9 V in low-power), 250-Ω typical output impedance (500 Ω low-power). The receiving AFE's unloaded input common-mode condition is 1.17–1.43 V in normal mode, 0.765–0.935 V in low-power. Output load is **Rload ≥25 kΩ on each leg** and capacitance ≤100 pF in the datasheet's Figure 11 model; place a 100-nF VDD bypass close to the microphone (pp.8–10 Tables 6–7/Fig.11). The >25-kΩ and capacitance conditions include cable/ESD/filter effects and must be checked under worst case. The bottom sound port recommends a 0.8-mm PCB hole and manufacturer pad/stencil guidance (pp.11–14). No standalone IM73A135 erratum was found in manufacturer material reviewed; lifecycle page marks the exact OPN active/preferred and AEC-Q103-003 qualification is **on demand**, not a Product V1 qualification claim.

### TLV320ADC5140 and microphone interface

`CONFIRMED` E-ADC is a 24-pin 4 × 4-mm WQFN with grounded exposed pad, specified −40 to +125 °C (pp.1, 4–5). Pins 6/7, 8/9, 10/11, 12/13 are IN1–IN4 P/M; 1 AVDD, 2 AREG, 3 VREF, 4 AVSS, 5 MICBIAS, 14 SHDNZ, 15/16 address or SPI, 17/18 SCL/SDA, 19 IOVDD, 20 GPIO1, 21 SDOUT, 22 BCLK, 23 FSYNC, 24 DREG. All four analog inputs can sample simultaneously, each selectable differential or single-ended, microphone or line (pp.1, 27–28). Per-pin typical input impedance is selectable 2.5, 10 or 20 kΩ; 2.5 kΩ is unavailable for DC coupling (p.28 Table 9). The device biases AC-coupled inputs internally; a coupling capacitor and selected impedance form a high-pass pole, and capacitor charging affects startup. Default VREF 2.75 V with AVDD 3.0–3.6 V gives 2 Vrms differential full scale; 1.8-V AVDD mode supports 1 Vrms differential (p.28 Table 10). Gain is 0–42 dB in 1-dB steps; DVC −100 to +27 dB (pp.1, 7–8).

`CONFLICTING`: even the ADC's highest selectable 20-kΩ input impedance is below the microphone's 25-kΩ **minimum output load resistance per leg**; direct connection cannot be approved from the two datasheets. A passive series/coupling network or high-impedance buffer is physically plausible, but the actual microphone loading, ADC full-scale/headroom, common-mode, noise, low-frequency corner/phase, EMI/ESD and cable capacitance must be solved together. The microphone's −38-dBV sensitivity corresponds to about 12.6 mVrms at 94 dB SPL; extrapolation toward 135 dB SPL is roughly 1.41 Vrms **only if the response remained linear**, which the 10%-THD AOP does not guarantee. Thus R4 must calculate and measure clipping margin at maximum SPL and chosen PGA gain. No final network values are selected. TI's Figure 172, pp.108–110, shows four AC-coupled differential analog microphones and provides a slave-TDM example; it is a topology reference, not validation of IM73A135 with the planned harness.

For the ADC, AVDD is 3.0–3.6 V or 1.65–1.95 V, IOVDD 1.65–1.95 or 3.0–3.6 V (E-ADC §7/§10). Four-channel 48-kHz, 3.3-V AVDD, PLL-on, DRE-off current is 21.3 mA typical, 23.6 mA with DRE on; IOVDD ~0.1 mA at 3.3 V under the stated test conditions (p.10). Do **not** use these as the 96-kHz maximum budget. VREF requires at least 1 µF to AVSS and DREG/AREG decoupling and thermal-pad grounding follow pp.108, 115–116. IOVDD/AVDD may rise in either order while SHDNZ stays low; release only after stable rails, then wait at least 2 ms before I²C/SPI transactions per p.115 §10. Sleep-to-active also needs its documented ≥1-ms wake period (pp.56, 109). SHDNZ is active-low hardware shutdown/reset (p.4). I²C slave addresses are 0x4C–0x4F (7-bit) from ADDR1:0; do not confuse the example's 0x98 write byte with a 7-bit address (p.57 Table 50).

ASI supports TDM, I²S and LJ; 16/20/24/32-bit words; master or slave; up to 64 programmable bus slots, with four useful analog output channels here (pp.1, 19–24). In slave mode BCLK/FSYNC auto-detection plus the internal PLL makes a separate MCLK optional, with 96 kHz × 128 = 12.288 MHz explicitly in Table 6 (pp.26–27). Master mode instead needs an external MCLK on GPIO/GPIx; **do not use ADC I²S/LJ master mode by default**, because TI flags a half-cycle-early FSYNC behavior (p.115 §9.3). Default HPF has a 12-Hz −3-dB point and is programmable; R4 must decide enable/coefficients after low-frequency phase evaluation (p.8 and §8.3.6). TI's 96-kHz low-latency and ultra-low-latency decimation filters specify 7.7/fs ≈80.2 µs and 3.7/fs ≈38.5 µs group delay respectively over their stated passbands (pp.44/48 Tables 35/41). These are **filter-only** figures. E-ADC-RATE p.6 Fig.3-3 specifically permits four analog channels at 96 kHz with AGC/DRE off; Fig.3-2 excludes four analog channels with AGC/DRE on. At 96 kHz the four-channel cell allows two biquads, but ANC should first evaluate a fixed-gain, no-AGC/DRE profile. The product needs a complete sample-to-speaker latency measurement later.

## 3. RT1062 controller, boot and ANC envelope

`CONFIRMED` E-MCU lists MIMXRT1062DVJ6A/B (12 × 12 mm, 0.8-mm pitch, 196 MAPBGA) and DVL6A/B (10 × 10 mm, 0.65-mm pitch, 196 MAPBGA) as 600-MHz consumer-grade alternatives (pp.6–7 Table 1). An industrial product family exists (E-MCU-I); exact temperature/MPN and silicon revision remain R3 selections. Cortex-M7 has an FPU, 32-KB instruction and 32-KB data cache, 1-MB on-chip RAM, 128-KB boot ROM; SRAM placement and cache coherence matter for DMA (E-MCU pp.2–3). The part has three SAI supporting I²S/TDM, 32-channel eDMA with DMAMUX, four I²C, two USB OTG, LPSPI and two FlexSPI instances, four watchdog blocks, JTAG/SWD CoreSight and GPIO (pp.2–3, 7, 13–17, 21). This is enough *resource class* for independent ADC receive and stereo transmit while firmware handles control/safety. AN12090 and the official SAI/eDMA multichannel example demonstrate multichannel DMA usage, not final Product V1 pin/resource allocation. E-MCU p.61 Table 50 sets 40-ns minimum SAI master BCLK cycle (≤25 MHz), so 12.288 MHz and 6.144 MHz candidates meet the controller's frequency bound. Exact SAI instance, data pins, frame sync routing, clock divider and timing margins are R3 work.

The MCU has PLL-based clocks and high-frequency XTALI plus low-frequency RTC_XTALI provisions (E-MCU pp.26, 33–35); crystal value, loading, audio PLL distribution and jitter budget must be frozen from E-MCU-HW/reference manual in R3/R4. GPIO supply banks are not all interchangeable: NVCC_GPIO is 3.0–3.6 V, NVCC_SD0/SD1 and NVCC_EMC have selectable 1.8/3.3-V operating ranges, and pins are not 5-V tolerant by assumption (E-MCU pp.25–26 Tables 10 and §4.2.1.3). All used *and unused* NVCC banks require power as specified. The 3.0–3.6-V DCDC_IN, 3.0–3.6-V VDD_HIGH_IN, 2.4–3.6-V VDD_SNVS_IN, 3.0–3.6-V VDDA_ADC_3P3 and internal core supply are distinct; at 600 MHz VDD_SOC_IN must satisfy 1.25–1.3 V (p.25 Table 10). The internal DCDC can produce the core rail from 3.3 V; this is not permission to omit its external network. Table 12 gives maximum DCDC_IN 110 mA, HIGH_IN 50 mA, SNVS_IN 250 µA under its stated max-power test, excluding I/O dynamic loading; calculate board rails and USB/flash separately. NXP requires SNVS first or tied to HIGH_IN, DCDC_PSWITCH delay after stable DCDC_IN, POR_B held until final rail, and no externally driven I/O when its bank is off (pp.30–31 §4.2.1). The 196-ball package mandates BGA escape, decoupling and X-ray/DFM review; E-MCU-HW Rev 7 remains an R4 mandatory read. Commercial junction range is 0–95 °C; thermal proof uses measured enclosure conditions (p.26).

### ANC feasibility envelope

Four input samples × 96,000 × 32 bit = **12.288 Mbit/s** bus payload; stereo output × 96,000 × 32 bit = **6.144 Mbit/s**. Six 32-bit samples per instant require 24 bytes, or **2.304 MB/s** raw transfer. With a block of B samples and ping-pong input/output buffers, base sample storage is at least `2 × B × 6 × 4 = 48B bytes`, before history, coefficients and control state. At 600 MHz there are 6,250 core cycles per 96-kHz frame nominally; this is an upper schedule envelope, **not** available FxLMS compute after DMA, cache, ISR, flash misses and safety work. A conservative single-channel FxLMS cost model is proportional to `F × L × U`, where F is number of adaptive filters, L taps and U updates per input frame; multichannel reference/error routing adds terms. R3 must supply F, L, block size B, arithmetic (float/fixed), update rate, permissible buffering, total RAM/code/cache placement, measured DSP/MAC cycles and input-to-output latency target. R4/EVT must profile worst-case eDMA and interrupt overlap and prove acoustic stability/safe mute. No final FxLMS capacity is claimed.

### External NOR and watchdog contracts

`CONFIRMED` E-MCU and E-MCU-MFG support external serial NOR through the boot-capable FlexSPI pad group, XIP and ROM/flashloader programming. R3 must select a quad-SPI NOR whose **1.8-V or 3.3-V supply matches the selected RT1062 NVCC_SD bank** and verify the exact supported read/erase/program command set, reset behavior, clock limit, SFDP/config block, package/footprint and write protection. Required signal class: CS#, SCK and IO0–IO3 plus VCC/GND; DQS is mode-dependent, so do not add it by assumption. The NXP EVK's ISSI IS25WP064AJBLE, 64 Mbit = 8 MiB, is a **1.8-V example candidate** (E-EVK and E-FLASH), not Product V1 MPN approval. A 3-V ISSI IS25LP064A family also exists (E-FLASH-3V), but boot support for that exact variant was not established here. An initial **≥8-MiB planning envelope** is reasonable for boot/application/calibration/possible recovery image because it matches the manufacturer EVK; it is **PROVISIONAL**, not an RT1062 minimum. R3 must replace it with a measured image + metadata + rollback budget and verify boot-ROM support and flash supply/pin mux; do not infer that every FlexSPI device boots.

The RT1062 internal WDOGs and EWM exist: NXP states WDOG resets the MCU while EWM can assert a pin to put external circuitry safe (E-MCU pp.16, 14). Neither alone proves the product requirement for an independent hardware path forcing amplifier safe during MCU power/clock/firmware failure. **R2 retains an independent external watchdog/supervisor or demonstrably equivalent independent hardware monitor as required.** It must power up inhibited, accept a valid heartbeat only after READY, force the Board B amplifier's active-low PDN/safe-state path on missed pulse, brownout, reset and disconnection, latch or hold safe until explicit recovery policy, and remain effective when RT1062 code, core clock or GPIO stops. Timeout, pulse window, polarity, pull network, reset/rearm and fault response time are `UNKNOWN` R3/R4 contracts; do not select the IC yet. EWM can provide an additional trigger, not replace the independent element without safety analysis.

## 4. Board B TAS5825M

E-AMP is a stereo digital-input, closed-loop Class-D in 32-pin 5 × 5-mm RHB VQFN with PowerPAD (pp.1, 4–5). In BTL mode, OUT_A± and OUT_B± are two independent differential outputs; speaker negatives are not ground (p.34). The recommended PVDD range is 4.5–26.4 V, DVDD 1.62–3.63 V (nominal 1.8 or 3.3 V), and minimum BTL load is 3.2 Ω (p.6 §7.3). Nominal 4 Ω is allowed only when actual impedance versus frequency and output-filter behavior respect this limit. Internal AVDD/GVDD/VR_DIG regulator pins are bypass-only and cannot power external circuits (pp.5, 28, 91–92). Digital input VIH ≥0.7 DVDD, VIL ≤0.3 DVDD (p.7); a shared 3.3-V domain is the simple candidate. DVDD current examples at 3.3 V/no speaker load range from 14.8 mA for 48-kHz housekeeping to 19.3 mA for 96-kHz housekeeping and 25.5 mA for full DSP; PVDD no-load hybrid-play examples are 20.5–29.5 mA at 13.5 V with different LC filters (p.7). These are *typical conditions*, not max power design values.

Audio pins are LRCLK/FS 12, SCLK/BCLK 13, SDIN 14; I²C SDA/SCL 15/16; ADR 8 determines address through a specified pull-down; PDN 17 is active-low hardware power-down; GPIO0–2 (9–11) may be configured as FAULTZ/WARNZ open drain or CMOS (pp.4–5, §9.5–9.6). The amplifier is a **clock slave** on its 3-wire interface, derives internal clocks from SCLK, and needs no MCLK (pp.1, 28–29). It supports I²S/LJ/RJ/TDM with 16/20/24/32-bit data; 96-kHz I²S uses 32 or 64 BCLK/fs but 32-bit stereo requires **64 × fs = 6.144 MHz**. The 96-kHz TDM modes are 128/256 fs, not necessary for a stereo link (p.29 Table 9-1). Stated BCLK bound is 24.576 MHz and LRCLK 32–192 kHz (p.7). Clock loss forces Hi-Z and automatic return after recovery (p.28); therefore **clock loss alone is not a latching product-safe policy**. Route independent hardware inhibition. PVDD/DVDD may rise in either order, but after stable supplies PDN rises, wait ≥5 ms, start clocks, configure Hi-Z/DSP, wait ≥5 ms and then Play; shutdown requires Hi-Z or PDN before rail removal and ≥6 ms in the stated 48-kHz/ramp example (pp.42–43). Product safe-state response may need faster hardware PDN assertion and must be measured.

Protection includes cycle-by-cycle limit, short-circuit shutdown, DC detection, PVDD UV/OV, overtemperature warnings and shutdown. Typical thresholds include 7.5-A overcurrent error, 6.5-A cycle limit, 28-V PVDD OV, 4.2-V PVDD UV, 160 °C OT error with 10 °C hysteresis and warning points 112/122/134/146 °C (pp.8, 43, 77–80). The OC shutdown <100 ns statement is conditional on a sufficiently severe short (p.43), not a general board fault-response guarantee. GPIO FAULTZ is configuration-dependent and fault registers need I²C read/clear; set a hardware safe default before configuration. The datasheet gives 14.5 W/ch at 14.4 V, 6 Ω, 1 kHz, 1% THD+N and 17.8 W/ch at 10%; it gives 0.03% at 1 W, 12 V, 6 Ω under its stated conditions (p.8). **These do not state Product V1 12-V/4-Ω continuous output capability**. Efficiency curves (pp.14, 91), output LC/inductor criteria (pp.82–84), close PVDD bypass, bootstrap capacitors and exposed-pad/contiguous copper thermal layout (pp.84, 92–94) are R4/R7 inputs. An inductorless mode exists for short inductive speaker wiring, but the product's harness/EMI may require LC; R4/R12 decide by measurement.

For future 12-V headroom, measure at the **minimum protected PVDD at the IC** and actual speaker impedance `Z(f)`: required `V_rms(f)` from acoustic A/B, `I_rms(f)=V_rms/|Z(f)|`, `P_real(f)=V_rms² Re(1/Z(f))`, simultaneous-stereo input power `P_in≈(P_L+P_R)/η + P_quiescent`, and device loss `P_loss≈P_in−P_L−P_R`. Include clipping/THD, DSP gain/limiter, supply drop/ripple, excursions, RMS and peak current, output filter saturation, enclosure temperature and ANC reserve. Ideal BTL sine ceiling `V_rms < PVDD/√2` is only a loose upper bound; use manufacturer curves and bench data for real headroom. No output power is frozen.

## 5. End-to-end digital audio and board interface

**Candidate common clock family (PROVISIONAL):** RT1062 clock tree produces coherent 96-kHz frame rates for two SAI modules. SAI RX drives 96-kHz FSYNC and 12.288-MHz BCLK to the four-channel ADC in slave TDM; SAI TX drives 96-kHz LRCLK and 6.144-MHz BCLK plus stereo data to the amp in slave I²S. Both clocks are integer multiples of 96 kHz and within cited endpoint tables. This is a *synchronous frequency architecture*, not yet a verified same-edge or zero-drift pin configuration. R3 must verify actual shared audio PLL/root, SAI master pin mux, frame alignment, cross-module synchronization, timing setup/hold including connector skew, DMA buffer transitions and latencies. A 48-kHz fallback is feasible electrically but changes ANC schedule/latency and is not selected.

## 7. Errata, lifecycle and controlled carry-forward

NXP's current listed i.MX RT1060 errata are **revision dependent** (E-MCU-ERR-A Rev 1.3; E-MCU-ERR-B Rev 1.1). For B silicon, `ERR050144` means SAI transmit `FCONT=1` with masked non-tail slots may fail FIFO recovery; use `FCONT=0` or prove an unaffected setting (p.10). `ERR011377` says FlexSPI DLL lock status can misreport, requiring a wait of at least 512 FlexSPI root-clock cycles after DLL lock before flash access; `ERR050538` identifies possible HAB boot failure on system reset if SJC_DISABLE fuse is blown, with JTAG pad pull workaround (pp.1, 11). `ERR011572/011573` affect core memory/cache/MPU behavior and must be addressed in firmware/cache/DMA review; `ERR010661` affects dual-OTG VBUS behavior (pp.1, 11). A silicon shares the SAI erratum but has a different errata set; R3 must choose the exact orderable silicon marking and re-evaluate **both** official errata, especially secure boot and FlexSPI. TI ADC's own datasheet p.115 §9.3 flags early FSYNC in I²S/LJ master mode and AGC limitation below 44.1 kHz; selecting slave TDM avoids the first configuration. The TAS5825M datasheet documents automatic clock-fault recovery (pp.28–29, 77–80), which is a product safety concern even if normal device behavior. No separate manufacturer errata document for the ADC, amplifier or microphone was found in the reviewed manufacturer listings; check current manufacturer advisories by exact ordering code before R4.

Manufacturer product pages list IM73A135 **active and preferred**, TI TLV320ADC5140 and TAS5825M **active**, and NXP RT1060 family **active** (E-IDs/lifecycle links in index).
