## 2. Board A microphone and AFE preflight

**Confirmed endpoint:** E-MIC pp.6–10 specifies normal-mode VDD 2.3–3.0 V, 2.75 V typical, 230 µA maximum per microphone under its stated ≤94-dB-SPL condition, 1.35-V nominal output common mode, 250-Ω typical output impedance, **at least 25 kΩ load on each output** and at most 100 pF load capacitance. Normal-mode start is 30 ms maximum after VDD reaches minimum. E-ADC p.28 Table 9 provides at most **20-kΩ typical** input impedance per leg. Direct mic→ADC loading is therefore contradictory; four identical isolation channels are required. The microphone 20-Hz low-frequency limit and measured acoustic phase must be respected; no reference/error role is assigned.

**Candidate only:** four dual OPA2320 unity-gain buffer packages, one buffer per differential leg, on a quiet 3.3-V analog rail. E-OPA documents rail-to-rail operation and 8.5 nV/√Hz typical voltage-noise density at 1 kHz. This can isolate each microphone from the ADC's input impedance. It is **not yet an approved network**: the exact OPA2320 order code, package, load, input/output swing at the selected temperature, output drive into coupling/ADC capacitance, rail transient, stability, cable/ESD capacitance, ESD clamp leakage, common-mode behavior and shutdown behavior are unverified as a whole. Mic port protection must preserve the ≤100-pF output load and ≥25-kΩ resistance. The manufacturer does not give a universal cable/ESD value.

Sensitivity −38 dBV at 94 dB SPL is `10^(−38/20) = 12.59 mVrms` differential (E-MIC p.6). An *illustrative linear extrapolation* by 41 dB to the published 135-dB-SPL, 10%-THD AOP gives 1.413 Vrms; allowing +1 dB sensitivity gives 1.585 Vrms. This is **not** a guaranteed maximum mic output or product SPL criterion. At unity gain, TI's 2-Vrms differential ADC full scale (AVDD 3.3 V, VREF 2.75 V; E-ADC p.28 Table 10) leaves only `20 log10(2/1.585) = 2.02 dB` illustrative electrical margin before coupling tolerance and PGA. ADC PGA must default 0 dB for a high-level capture mode; any positive gain reduces that margin. With +1 dB sensitivity, a balanced leg's illustrative peak is `1.585 × √2 / 2 = 1.121 V` around mic common mode, but output common-mode limits, clipping at AOP, cable pickup and active-buffer swing still need a guaranteed worst-case audit. No final high-SPL or noise-floor claim follows from this extrapolation.

For an AC-coupled ADC leg, `f_c = 1/(2π R_in C_eff)`. The nominal 20-kΩ setting would give 7.96 Hz at 1 µF and 0.796 Hz at 10 µF; at 80 Hz their single-pole phase shifts are approximately 5.68° and 0.57°. **Neither is frozen** because 20 kΩ is typical rather than a warranted minimum, effective capacitor value versus DC bias and temperature is unknown, and source resistance/filter topology adds poles. The ADC default 12-Hz HPF (E-ADC p.8) would itself shift phase roughly 8.5° at 80 Hz if left enabled; its exact digital response/register setting must be selected. The four channels must use the same analog values and firmware profile. Required R5 values still absent: coupling type/value/voltage/dielectric/tolerance, input bias path, series/filter/ESD components, ADC input impedance setting, PGA and HPF setting, and anti-alias/EMI response. No reproducible full-path simulation exists because these values are not frozen.

Noise sanity check, **typical-only and condition-limited**: mic 73-dB(A) SNR at 94 dB SPL implies `12.59 mV / 10^(73/20) ≈ 2.82 µVrms` A-weighted self-noise. A white 8.5-nV/√Hz OPA320 estimate over an arbitrary 20-kHz rectangular band gives ~1.20 µVrms per leg, ~1.70 µVrms differential, before 1/f noise, resistor/cable noise and bandwidth shaping. TI ADC 107-dB typical SNR at its specified **48-kHz, 0-dB-gain, A-weighted** condition corresponds to ~8.93 µVrms referred to 2-Vrms full scale. These dissimilar conditions cannot form a 96-kHz guaranteed noise budget. For any proposed resistor, use `e_n = √(4kTRB)` and the actual transfer function; a 1-kΩ resistor over 20 kHz contributes ~0.58 µVrms at 300 K before topology gains. A 96-kHz, four-channel, complete-band noise and phase simulation/bench plan is required to close `PV1-R1-001`.

## 5. Board B input, 5 V and amplifier preflight

Board B owns protected external nominal 12-V-class input, amp PVDD, a buck-generated 5-V feed to Board A, local 3.3-V DVDD and local default-off amplifier gate. E-AMP p.6 allows PVDD 4.5–26.4 V and DVDD 1.62–3.63 V; actual speaker headroom remains EVT. `P_in ≈ (P_left + P_right)/η + P_quiescent`; use actual frequency-dependent speaker impedance, simultaneous output, amplifier efficiency, supply drop and protection trip tolerance to size input/protected PVDD. The ideal BTL sine ceiling `V_rms < PVDD/√2` is only an upper bound. **No adapter tolerance/transient envelope, peak Board A current, speaker electrical limit, eFuse trip point, TVS clamp, reverse-protection device, 5-V buck MPN, local 3.3-V regulator or thermal limit is frozen.** Selecting a nominal 12-V-only buck would risk the unresolved headroom adjustment; select only after the allowed future input range is bounded. E-AMP's 7.5-A typical overcurrent and 28-V typical PVDD overvoltage are device protections, not a Board B input protection specification.

The 5-V connector budget must use `V_A,min = V_B,min − I_A,max (R_out + R_contact_power + R_contact_return + R_trace)` and account for contact aging, inrush and ripple. R3's two 5-V and four GND contacts are a **minimum inventory**, not proven current sharing or voltage-drop approval. No maximum Board A current, allowable 5-V receiving range or resistance budget is known. Any selected buck must be checked using **guaranteed** VIN/VOUT/load/temperature conditions, feedback reference and divider tolerance, min/max regulation, inductor saturation, output capacitor effective value, thermal dissipation and PGOOD behavior. None can be calculated to a valid final margin with the absent input/load envelope. The same applies to every Board A regulator. The legacy regulator/supervisor circuits were not reused.

## 6. Board-to-board DC/timing and hardware safety

The [interface contract](../../state/board_to_board_interface.json) retains ten required classes: 2× 5 V, 4× GND, BCLK, LRCLK, audio data, SDA, SCL, AMP_MUTE/ENABLE, AMP_FAULT and PGOOD; MCLK and optional service are excluded. Audio is A→B, I²C SDA bidirectional, SCL A→B, AMP_MUTE A→B, FAULT and PGOOD B→A. A shared low-impedance return with adjacent clock/power GND is required; no arbitrary split in cross-board ground. Board B PVDD and speaker switching loops stay local to B, away from A's mic AFE. Mechanical connector, pin order and current-rated contact family wait for R7, but that does not waive R4 electrical current and timing proof.

At 3.3-V nominal TAS DVDD, E-AMP p.7 requires `VIH ≥0.7×DVDD` and `VIL ≤0.3×DVDD`; across its 1.62–3.63-V allowed range the worst rising threshold can be 2.541 V at 3.63 V. A 3.3-V nominal output is **not** a worst-case VOH guarantee. For each output the required inequalities are `VOH_min(source, I_load) − VIH_max(receiver) > 0` and `VIL_min(receiver) − VOL_max(source, I_sink) > 0`, with receiver absolute maximum and unpowered clamp current checked separately. I²C additionally needs pull-up ownership, R/C rise time, bus capacitance, low-level current and isolation during unequal rail startup. For 6.144-MHz BCLK, period is 162.76 ns; frame is 10.417 µs. The sender's data-valid/clock edge, pad delay, connector flight/skew, receiver setup/hold and loaded rise/fall must fit the appropriate half-cycle. The R2 protocol match does **not** supply these loaded margins. Each active signal now has explicit `null` DC/timing margins in the JSON audit; no fabricated PASS is recorded. Board B MCLK is not required by TAS5825M's three-wire serial port (E-AMP pp.28–29).

Independent safe-state requirement is retained. Functional Boolean contract: `PDN_RELEASE = B_POWER_GOOD ∧ A_PRESENT ∧ WATCHDOG_VALID ∧ MCU_ENABLE_REQUEST ∧ NO_LOCAL_HARD_FAULT`; every unknown/open/low input inhibits PDN. The Board B pull/gate must hold PDN low with A disconnected or unpowered, B logic ramping, watchdog missing, and MCU reset; firmware alone cannot release it. A must send a heartbeat only after self-test, while B must obtain a power-good and fault decision locally. `AMP_FAULT` is advisory to A until TAS GPIO configuration; a B local fault path must inhibit independently. A communication failure policy must have a bounded timeout independent of indefinite I²C polling. **No exact independent watchdog/supervisor/gate MPN, polarity-level proof, timeout, latch/rearm policy, or PDN response time is selected.** Any supervisor must prove `V_valid_min > V_RISE,max` and `V_invalid_max < V_FALL,min`, including reference and divider tolerances, hysteresis, delay, ripple and load; neither margin exists yet. This keeps `PV1-R1-011` open.

The hardware dependency order is DC input→protection→5 V and B local logic→A rails→reference/MCU reset→flash ROM boot→ADC clock/config→B amplifier config in PDN released but DSP safe state→READY/watchdog qualification→controlled PLAY. A fault, PGOOD loss, disconnection or watchdog timeout drives B's local gate to PDN low without firmware execution; recovery returns through boot/self-test rather than automatic clock-fault replay. This is a proposed ordering, **not an implemented verified graph**. With no gate/supervisor components, powered-off clamp/driver behavior and all threshold values remain unproven.

### Public-evidence calculations that narrow, but do not close, the blockers

`validation/r4_closure_calculations.py` reproduces these **conditional** figures. NXP CEC Rev 4 Table 22 gives 3.0-V-bank GPIO `VOH >= 2.85 V` and `VOL <= 0.15 V` at the stated <=1-mA drive. A Board B `SN74LVC1G125` at 3.0-3.6 V needs `VIH >= 2.0 V`, `VIL <= 0.8 V`, yielding 0.85-V HIGH and 0.65-V LOW input margins. With its output loaded <=100 microA, `VOH >= VCC-0.1 V` and `VOL <=0.1 V`. The buffer and TAS must use the **same** local Board B 3.0-3.6-V rail: at the common 3.0-V lower corner, TAS `VIH =0.7 DVDD` and `VIL =0.3 DVDD` give 0.8-V HIGH and LOW margins. Separate 3.0-V buffer and 1.62-V TAS supplies could violate the TAS `DVDD+0.5-V` digital-input absolute maximum. **Caveat:** TI labels TAS electrical characteristics as 25-C free-air values, not a guaranteed full-temperature logic threshold. These are conditional operating-point calculations, not a temperature-corner proof. Direct A-to-unpowered-B connection is invalid. The LVC buffer has `Ioff <=10 microA` with VCC=0 and a 5.5-V input/output; use is a candidate isolation approach, with OE/power-ramp state and board-level clamp path still to prove.

For the amp link, `96,000 x 2 x 32 = 6.144 MHz`, period `162.760 ns`. NXP Table 50 permits a 40/60-percent master BCLK and 15-ns maximum clock-to-TXD-valid, with 0-ns minimum clock-to-TXD-invalid. TAS p.10 requires 8-ns data setup and hold and 8-ns LRCLK/SCLK separation. The 125-C `SN74LVC1G125` 30/50-pF propagation range is 1.0-4.7 ns. If total differential interconnect/receiver edge uncertainty is **bounded to 5 ns**, the shortest 40-percent phase is 65.104 ns, giving conditional data setup `65.104-15-(4.7-1)-5-8 = 33.404 ns` and hold `65.104-(4.7-1)-5-8 = 48.404 ns`. A complete proof still needs NXP's exact configured launch edge, LRCLK phase, source output timing at the real capacitive load, mechanical interconnect bound, buffer OE sequencing and TAS temperature characterization. The analogous ADC link is `96,000 x 4 x 32 = 12.288 MHz`; exact SAI1 framing/PLL remains open.

The TI `TCA9800` is a possible I2C isolation device, but its specified maximum SCL is **100 kHz** (SCPS264B p.7), so a future 400-kHz assumption would be false. Its B-side internal current source forbids external B-side pull-ups; A-side ownership, receiver leakage and full off-state proof remain unresolved. The TI `TPS54334` has a feedback reference of 0.788-0.812 V across the stated VIN 4.2-28 V and TJ -40..150 C (SLUSC26A p.5); this makes it a stronger 5-V buck *candidate* than a reference whose accuracy is specified only at VIN=12 V, but no divider, inductor or current/thermal proof was completed. `TPS7A2028PDQNR` is a possible 2.8-V microphone LDO: TI's +/-1.5-percent condition gives 2.758-2.842 V, within the mic's 2.3-3.0-V range, **only if** VIN remains at least the nominal 2.8 V plus 0.3 V (3.1 V), IOUT stays 1-300 mA, and TI's capacitor/temperature conditions hold. A generic 3.0-V-min 3V3 rail cannot meet that guaranteed VIN. This candidate is therefore not a frozen rail circuit.

### 10.1 Owner requirements and provisional engineering boundary

The PoC 12 V/10 A adapter is **not** a Product V1 requirement. Retain 12 V nominal only as an engineering baseline. The final adapter voltage and model remain TBD after protected rail, thermal, speaker and acoustic validation. Reverse polarity, OVP, OCP and short-circuit protection remain required. For screening the power design, use an **8–18 V candidate operating range at the external input**, subject to protection drop and regulation; this is a proposed engineering envelope, not a production rating. The endpoint limits themselves are TAS5825M PVDD 4.5–26.4 V and DVDD 1.62–3.63 V (E-AMP p.6); the chosen buck, eFuse, TVS and reverse-polarity circuit must keep their respective pins and PVDD inside guarantee and fault limits over input tolerances and transients. The candidate 8–18 V range is not yet validated by a complete selected power circuit.

For an **electrical sizing screen only**, two ideal full-scale BTL sine channels at 3.2-ohm minimum permitted load give `P_out,ideal <= V_PVDD^2 / 3.2`: 20 W at 8 V, 45 W at 12 V and 101.25 W at 18 V. This ignores amplifier loss, voltage drop, thermal limits, gain setting and output limiting; it is neither attainable production power nor an acoustic permission. With an *unverified conservative efficiency assumption* of 70%, an illustrative 10-W Board A budget, an assumed 85%-efficient 5-V buck and 25% current allowance, the screening formula is `I_adapter >= 1.25 × [V_PVDD/(3.2 × 0.70) + 10 W/(0.85 × V_input)]`; at 12 V it gives approximately **7.9 A**, at 18 V approximately **10.9 A**. The assumed Board A load and efficiencies are not guaranteed; neither figure approves an adapter or the eFuse rating. A valid R4 power budget requires per-device worst-case current, amplifier continuous/transient/limiter operating point, protection and buck losses, and thermal testing. The PoC 10-A label is not used as evidence.

The production safety document requires independent output limiting, watchdog, hardware mute, fault-default silence and controlled release. The owner supplied `<=10 ms` as a **provisional circuit-engineering mute target only**. It is not a certified response limit, acoustic acceptance limit, or compliance claim. The final production response criterion and numeric baby SPL acceptance remain open for later independent safety/acoustic evaluation (exposure duration, geometry, peak/transient, clinical input). E-AMP p.7 lists a 10-ms turn-off figure **excluding volume ramp** at its stated test condition; it cannot establish a worst-case fault-to-silence bound under all rails, temperatures, firmware states and speaker loads. The previously considered TPS3431 has a fastest *specified recommended-capacitor typical* timeout of 62.74 ms (E-WATCHDOG p.4), so it cannot itself detect a crashed heartbeat within 10 ms. ### 10.2 NXP ROM, resources and clocks now verified

E-MCU-RM4 pp.193–200 establishes BOOT_MODE[1:0] `00` Boot From Fuses, `01` Serial Downloader, `10` Internal Boot, `11` reserved; `00` with BT_FUSE_SEL=0 enters downloader, and `10` with BT_FUSE_SEL=0 uses GPIO boot configuration. Production fuse boot is recommended by NXP, but its one-time provisioning and security policy must be controlled. ROM boot from FlexSPI A uses SD_B1_06/07/08–11 for CS/CLK/IO[0:3]; FlexSPI B cannot directly supply the first ROM image. E-MCU-RM4 pp.210–214 gives a 512-byte FCB at flash offset zero and 30-MHz initial read. E-MCU-HW7 pp.9 and 14–15 requires valid BOOT_CFG[0:11] levels until fuses supersede them and documents USB1/LPUART1 downloader. The exact flash manufacturer order code, FCB command/LUT, strap resistor matrix, fuse provisioning and recovery test remain absent. These now are **implementation gaps**, not missing NXP-source gaps. The partial but collision-free pad map is in `state/rt1062_pin_resource_map.json`.

E-MCU-HW7 pp.2–6 supplies the current power/decoupling and DCDC requirements: SNVS power must precede or tie to VDD_HIGH_IN; DCDC_PSWITCH uses a 5–15-ms RC and must reach 1.5 V at least 1 ms after DCDC input stability; POR_B stays asserted until all rails are valid. The internal DCDC needs a 4.7–10-uH external inductor with saturation >1 A and ESR <0.2 ohm and 33 uF total output capacitance. Its Table 2 lists pin-domain capacitor quantities and values. E-MCU-HW7 p.7 requires a 24-MHz fundamental crystal (ESR <80 ohm, CL <=12 pF) for the SDK clock plan, with 2.2-Mohm XTALI-to-ground bias. Its pp.7–10 specify SWD default, JTAG_MOD/TRSTB 4.7-kohm low terminations, LPUART1 10-kohm pullups, boot-strap bias and POR_B supervision guidance. Exact production crystal, effective capacitors, reset circuit and the whole rail-load budget are still missing.

An exact *frequency* plan follows E-MCU-RM4 pp.1028, 1052, 1056–1058, 2000 and 2016: 24-MHz reference; PLL4 `DIV_SELECT=32, NUM=96, DENOM=125` gives 786.432-MHz VCO; post-divide 4 gives 196.608 MHz. SAI1/SAI2 root selection PLL4 with PRED=2, PODF=4 gives 24.576 MHz to each; SAI1 RX bit-clock DIV=0 yields 12.288 MHz for four 32-bit slots at 96 kHz; SAI2 TX DIV=1 yields 6.144 MHz for two 32-bit slots at 96 kHz. `validation/r4_closure_calculations.py` asserts this arithmetic. The RM p.1977 says synchronous mode across **different SAI peripherals is unsupported**. A shared PLL locks average rates but does not define relative frame phase; DMA buffering, start alignment, drift/fault handling and total latency must be specified and tested. The audio plan is frequency-coherent, not yet a complete registered/DMA implementation contract.

TI E-ADC p.12 specifies SDOUT delay up to 18 ns at **25 °C and 20-pF load**. E-MCU-R4 SAI receive setup is 15 ns. At 12.288 MHz, a nominal half-cycle is 40.69 ns, leaving `40.69−18−15=7.69 ns` **before** clock/data skew, or 2.69 ns with a proposed 5-ns skew. This is not a full-temperature or loaded guarantee. The ADC input timing, interconnect and any buffer propagation must be proven or the framing/edge plan amended explicitly before R5. The amplified link's conditional calculations in section 9 similarly do not prove every operating corner.

One concrete timing mitigation remains available without changing the 96-kHz sample rate: TI E-ADC pp.20–22 permits 24-bit output words and matching 24-bit TDM slots. Four slots would use 9.216-MHz BCLK. E-MCU-RM4's PLL/divider equations permit 24 MHz × (36 + 108/125) = 884.736 MHz PLL4 VCO, `/4 = 221.184 MHz`; SAI1 root `/3/4 = 18.432 MHz` and RX BCLK `/2 = 9.216 MHz`, while SAI2 root `/3/3 = 24.576 MHz` and TX BCLK `/4 = 6.144 MHz`. Under the same *25 °C, 20-pF and assumed 5-ns skew* conditions, ADC setup room would grow to `54.25−18−15−5 = 16.25 ns`. This is a **candidate R3 framing amendment, not a frozen R4 contract**: full-temperature timing, clock/data edge and DMA/word-packing tests remain necessary. It cannot be substituted silently for the present four-by-32-bit baseline.

The seven findings therefore remain OPEN and blocking. They were not downgraded to make the gate pass.
